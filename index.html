01 — What we work on

Four questions, worked in the open

Volatility is the most forecastable thing in finance and still routinely forecast badly. Most of the damage happens before any model is fitted: in how the target is measured and how the result is evaluated.

01

Measurement

Realized variance, realized kernels, microstructure noise, jump separation. You cannot forecast a quantity you have not defined properly.

02

The surface

Smile, skew and term structure; arbitrage-free parameterisation; what the surface implies about the risk-neutral distribution.

03

Forecasting

Horizon by horizon, with honest out-of-sample protocol, sensible loss functions and significance testing rather than a single flattering backtest.

04

Regimes

Clustering, long memory, roughness and structural breaks — why persistence estimated in calm markets fails exactly when it matters.

02 — Model zoo

The models we build on

Four families, each answering a different question. Nothing here is a silver bullet; most of the skill is in knowing which family the problem belongs to.

Conditional variance

Discrete time, daily returns

  • ARCH Engle, 1982
  • GARCH Bollerslev, 1986
  • EGARCH Nelson, 1991
  • GJR-GARCH Glosten–Jagannathan–Runkle, 1993
  • TGARCH Zakoian, 1994
  • APARCH / fGARCH Hentschel, 1995
  • IGARCH · EWMA RiskMetrics
  • GARCH-MIDAS mixed frequency
  • MF2-GARCH Conrad & Engle, 2025

Realized measures

Intraday data, observable target

  • Realized variance sum of squared returns
  • Realized kernels Barndorff-Nielsen et al.
  • Bipower variation jump-robust
  • HAR-RV Corsi, 2009
  • HAR-RV-J / CHAR jump and continuous split
  • Realized GARCH Hansen et al., 2012
  • HEAVY Shephard & Sheppard

Continuous time

Pricing, surfaces, roughness

  • Black–Scholes constant vol baseline
  • Local volatility Dupire, 1994
  • Heston 1993
  • SABR Hagan et al., 2002
  • Bates · 3/2 · CEV jumps and elasticity
  • SVI / SSVI Gatheral & Jacquier
  • Rough Bergomi, RFSV Gatheral, Jaisson & Rosenbaum

Machine learning

Where it helps, and where it does not

  • XGBoost gradient-boosted trees
  • LightGBM leaf-wise boosting
  • Random forest variance reduction
  • LSTM / GRU recurrent sequence models
  • Temporal Fusion Transformer attention, covariates
  • N-BEATS deep basis expansion
  • Gaussian processes uncertainty by construction
  • SHAP attribution, not explanation

A field guide to the GARCH family

ARCH begat GARCH, and GARCH begat a zoo. What each extension is actually fixing — asymmetry, persistence, long memory — and how to tell which one your data is asking for.
ForecastingMethod15 min

Forecasting realized volatility, and what actually beats HAR

Corsi's three-lag regression is embarrassingly hard to beat. We set up an honest horse race against it and look at where the added complexity of rough volatility and deep learning earns its keep.

Gradient boosting for volatility: where XGBoost wins

Trees cannot extrapolate, and volatility spikes are extrapolation. A practical account of feature design, target transforms and the leakage traps that make backtests look far better than they are.
